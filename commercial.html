FOR BUSINESS PROPERTIES

A COMMERCIAL ROOF SHOULD HAVE A PLAN, NOT JUST A PATCH.

Discuss practical maintenance and repair decisions for flat, low-slope or pitched commercial roofing systems. The exact roof assembly and available services must be confirmed on inspection.

Document the existing system

A site review should identify the roof covering, drainage paths, penetrations, accessible seams, patches and visible deterioration. Ask for photos and written observations rather than relying on an estimate with one line item.

Plan around operations

Commercial projects may need access coordination, noise planning, debris controls, delivery scheduling and tenant communication. Agree on the working schedule and any interruptions before the project begins.

Repair, maintenance or re-roof

An isolated leak might be repairable; widespread deterioration can justify a broader scope. Ask how recommendations account for water intrusion, remaining service life and future maintenance.

Keep useful records

Save reports, before-and-after photos, material specifications, maintenance dates and the final invoice. Consistent records make budgeting and future roof assessments more straightforward.

Need a roof-specific answer? Request an inspection and ask for a written scope before approving work.

ROOFING EXPERTISE

WHAT TO EXPECT FROM A COMMERCIAL ROOF REVIEW

Identify the roof system

A site visit should identify whether the building has a built-up, modified bitumen, foam, single-ply, or another roof assembly before selecting a repair method.

Find vulnerabilities early

Commercial roof inspections often focus on seams, flashing, drains, rooftop units, penetrations and evidence of standing water or previous patching.

Plan for business continuity

Discuss building access, safety restrictions, occupant impacts, the scope of repairs and a practical schedule before work begins.
